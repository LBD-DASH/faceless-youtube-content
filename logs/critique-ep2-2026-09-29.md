# Critique: Episode 2 script (`scripts/2026-09-29.md`)

Reviewed for bedtime pacing, narration word count, and kid-safety, per the 2026-09-29 SHARED_UPDATES instruction.

## Word count
Counted the "Full voiceover script" section only (narration lines, excluding bracketed sound cues and **Visual:** notes): **about 1,114 words**. That sits inside the script's own 1,000-1,250 target, toward the lower-middle of the range. At a slow bedtime pace (roughly 110-130 words per minute) that's close to 9-10 minutes of narration alone, which matches the stated 8-10 minute runtime once pauses (the star-counting beat) and visual holds are added. No change needed here, just confirming the target was hit.

## Kid-safety
No distress-bait found. The owl is consistently calm (amber eyes, "soft night lamp with feathers"), the hillside stumble has "no hurt cry," and the doubt beat (9) keeps Tilly physically beside Baylin the whole time so the low point is never a lonely one. Good as is.

## Fix suggestions (numbered)

1. **Trim the repeated moral statement.** The lesson is stated twice almost back to back: beat 11 ("The answer was never to rush the moon... trust it, and wait...") and beat 12 ("Waiting is a quiet kind of bravery"). Cut or shorten the beat 11 version and let beat 12's direct-to-viewer line land as the single takeaway, so it doesn't feel preachy.

2. **Vary the repeated "worry" line.** The hook ("worry cannot make it come any faster") and beat 3 ("Worrying in bed will not bring the moon any faster") are near-duplicates. Reword one so the callback feels like an echo rather than a copy-paste.

3. **Simplify the owl's dialogue for the young end of the 4-8 range.** "The moon rises when it is ready, not when it is called. Worry cannot pull it up. Only time can." is fine for a 7-8 year old but abstract for a 4-5 year old. Consider splitting into two shorter, more concrete sentences, e.g. "The moon rises when it is ready. Not when it is called."

4. **Swap "Fetching" for a simpler word.** Beat 3's "Fetching it might" uses a slightly formal word next to otherwise simple bedtime vocabulary. "Going to get it" or "Finding it" reads more naturally at this age.

5. **Add a second interactive pause.** Only beat 8 (counting stars) has an explicit on-screen child-participation cue. For a 4-8 audience, a second short pause, for example a "can you spot the silver glow too?" beat around beat 10, would help hold attention through the second half without adding new plot.

6. **Confirm sound design avoids any startle cue on the stumble (beat 4).** The script already says "no hurt cry," which is good; flag for whoever builds the audio track that the stumble sound effect itself should stay soft (a low thud, not a sharp crack) to match.

7. **Tighten beat 6's shout.** "Moon! Moon! Please hurry!" repeated loudly is intentional as an Ep 1 callback (noted in the originality check), but at bedtime a second all-caps-energy shout beat this early in the episode risks re-energising a child who is meant to be winding down. Consider having Baylin call once, not twice, before the "nothing happened" beat.

## Overall
Script is close to production-ready on pacing, word count and safety grounds. The suggestions above are polish, not blockers. Kevin's approval decisions (placeholders, narrator, art style) remain the actual gate, per the `[KEVIN] decision flags` at the bottom of `scripts/2026-09-29.md`.
