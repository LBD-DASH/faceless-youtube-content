# Episode 6 critique: Princess Baylin and the Market of Small Kindnesses

Source: `scripts/2026-10-03.md`. Text only, no rewrite. Narration word counts are per-beat, counted from the voiceover lines only (visual notes and bracketed sound/cue directions excluded).

## 1. The close (Beat 12, 9:25-10:00) repeats the Ep 5 pacing problem, and runs even faster

Beat 12 carries roughly 150 words of narration in its 35-second slot, about 260 words per minute, against 90-120 words per minute everywhere else in the episode. It stacks more jobs than Ep 5's close did: walking home, bed, the spoken thank-you line, the on-screen trilingual ritual text, the reflective sharing question, two extra examples ("Perhaps a soft word... Perhaps a turn... Perhaps something small and free"), a 32-word single-sentence Ep 7 tease, and the goodnight sign-off. This is the same finding already logged in `logs/critique-ep5-2026-10-02.md` finding 1, now recurring in the very next episode, so it is worth treating as a pattern in the close-beat template rather than a one-off. Fix: split into two beats (extending runtime by about 30-45 seconds is fine against the 8-10 minute target) - one short beat for walking home, bed, and the thank-you ritual; a second calmer beat for the reflective question and Ep 7 tease. If runtime can't move, cut the two extra sharing examples ("a turn... something small and free") since the question alone already invites the child to answer, and shorten the Ep 7 tease sentence by about half.

## 2. The hook (0:00-0:25) runs faster than Ep 5's hook did, at roughly 165 words per minute

That is the fastest beat in the episode by a wide margin, well above the 90-120 words per minute used through the rest of the episode. Ep 5's hook was already flagged at 125 wpm as "a bit brisk"; this one is noticeably faster still. Fix: trim a few words, for example "Come with us to a gentle market morning, where kindness learns to share in small ways" could lose "to a gentle market morning" since Beat 1 re-establishes the setting in its first line anyway, or slow the reading pace in direction notes instead of cutting words.

## 3. A production note has leaked into spoken narration text in Beat 8 (Ripple)

The line reads: "An illustrated child friend (never a real child's face) offered a ribbon for a basket handle." The parenthetical is a safety/production instruction for the illustrator, not something the narrator should read aloud, but it sits inside the narration paragraph rather than in the `[bracket]` cue line or the **Visual:** note below it. Fix: move "(never a real child's face)" down into the Beat 8 Visual note (which already says "gentle animal friends. Never dense crowd") and change the narration line to "A neighbour's child offered a ribbon for a basket handle" or similar, so nothing inside the spoken narration block risks being read by a narrator or TTS pass.

## 4. Mid-cue and child-pause alignment (Beat 7) is correctly built, no fix needed

The on-screen cue "Share... soft and free," the baked-in 3-5 second pause, and the surrounding slower effective pace (about 98 words per minute once the pause is accounted for) land together as intended: cue and pause open the beat, the small actions follow, and the whispered callback line closes it. Confirmed good, not re-flagged in a future pass.

## 5. Kid-safety on the spill beat (Beat 3) passes

The spill is explicitly de-escalated inside the line itself ("Nothing broke. No one was hurt... She was flustered, not scared.") rather than left to sit as unresolved embarrassment, and the visual note repeats the same guardrail ("No broken glass. No distress."). This is the right template for the series; no fix needed.

Flagging this so findings 1 and 2 read as pacing/distribution problems within the episode, not a total-length problem. The 10:00 runtime against roughly 1,165 words averages to about 117 words per minute overall, consistent with the "8 to 10 minutes, calm bedtime pace" note at the top of the script, and close to the ~1,190-word estimate already logged for this episode in `SHARED_UPDATES.md`.

Once the native-speaker check lands and dubbed VO is produced, re-check this beat's timing specifically for AF/ZU, since translated lines are rarely the same length as the English and this beat currently has the least room in the episode to absorb a longer one.
