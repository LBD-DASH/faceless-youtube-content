## Policy check (YouTube rules that matter for this channel, checked 27 Sep 2026)

**1. "Made for kids" (MFK): required, and it switches a lot off.**
- Creators must set the audience honestly, and YouTube can override the setting. On MFK videos YouTube **turns off**: personalised ads, comments, the notification bell, save to playlist and Watch Later, the Miniplayer, autoplay on home, **cards and end screens**, video watermarks, **merchandise and ticketing**, channel memberships, Super Chat and Super Stickers, the donate button, and live chat. MFK channels also lose Posts and notifications. Ads can still run, but they're contextual, not personalised. Sources: https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/9527654 · https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/9632097
- **What this means for us:** there are **no end screens or cards to chain episodes together**, **no merch shelf** for Baylin products, **no comments** for audience feedback, and lower ad revenue. Episodes have to earn watch time by themselves: strong openings, series playlists on the channel page, and consistent titles. Books and merch can't be sold through YouTube's merch features on MFK videos. Whether a plain link in the description is acceptable or useful is unverified, so check before planning around it.
- **Kids quality principles decide monetisation.** A channel with "a strong focus on low-quality made-for-kids content" can be suspended from YPP. The low-quality signals are: heavily promotional, encouraging negative behaviour, deceptively educational, **hard to follow** ("often the result of mass production or autogeneration"), sensational or misleading (including **keyword stuffing**), and strange use of children's characters. Sources: https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392 · https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/10774223
  - So: every episode needs a clear beginning, middle and end, and clear audio. Titles must not be keyword-stuffed. Don't hard-sell books or merch inside the episode, because "heavily promotional" is a low-quality signal.

**2. AI (altered/synthetic) content disclosure.**
- Creators must disclose AI content that is **photorealistic** and meaningfully altered or generated. **Non-realistic or animated** AI content doesn't need disclosure. The help page's examples of content that doesn't need disclosing include "someone riding a unicorn through a fantastical world" and "production assistance, like using generative AI tools to create or improve a video outline, script, thumbnail". **AI-generated music is listed as needing disclosure.** Disclosing "won't limit a video's audience or impact its eligibility to earn money". YouTube may auto-label AI content, including files with C2PA metadata. Creators who consistently fail to disclose can face penalties, including YPP suspension. Source: https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/14328491
- Since May 2026 YouTube has automatically applied labels when it detects "significant photorealistic AI". Labels for animated or unrealistic content sit in the expanded description. Source: https://blog.youtube/news-and-events/improving-ai-labels-viewers-creators/
- **Our rule:** storybook illustrations are non-realistic, so AI help with the art doesn't strictly require disclosure. **If any AI music is used, disclose.** A synthetic TTS narrator isn't named in either list. Because disclosing costs nothing in reach or money, **set "AI use: Yes" whenever an AI voice or AI music is used**, and say so in the description. **[KEVIN] final call.**

**3. YPP eligibility and the "inauthentic content" policy.**
- **Current bar:** 1,000 subscribers plus either 4,000 public watch hours in the last 12 months, or 10M Shorts views in 90 days. Shorts-feed hours don't count towards the 4,000. Review covers the whole channel and typically takes about a month. You also need 2-Step Verification and AdSense for YouTube. Source: https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/72851
- **From 1 Feb 2027, new applicants** need 1,000 subscribers plus either **8,000 watch hours in 365 days or 20M Shorts views in 90 days**. Existing partners aren't affected. Source: https://blog.youtube/news-and-events/youtube-partner-program-updates-2027-new-opportunities-earn/
- **Inauthentic content** (renamed from "repetitious" on 15 Jul 2025) covers content that is "mass-produced, generic, repetitive" or feels "interchangeable from video to video" or "produced using a template". Such content isn't eligible for monetisation. Source: https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392
- **16 July 2026 clarification:** three buckets can't be monetised: (1) generic, repetitive or template-based content, including AI, CGI or template content "with little variation from video to video"; (2) off-putting, distressing or emotionally manipulative content; (3) AI personas discussing sensitive topics. Source: https://techcrunch.com/2026/07/20/youtube-clarifies-policies-around-ai-slop-and-upsetting-videos/
- **The risk for us:** "AI bedtime story" channels are exactly the pattern bucket (1) targets. Our defences: every episode has its own plot, setting and type of resolution; the art is human-directed, with a consistent cast; there's a real narrator (ideally family); Kevin reviews every episode; the stories are real trilingual adaptations; there's a recurring world with character growth. Also avoid distress-bait ("Baylin is lost and crying!") in titles and thumbnails, because that's bucket (2).

### Narrator decision brief (for Kevin)
Kevin's own voice: no cost, minimal extra time since he is already involved in review, and the strongest trust and authenticity signal for parents. Lowest inauthentic-content risk, since a real, consistent human narrator is one of the defences YouTube's policy explicitly rewards. Downside: recording and re-take time per episode, and voice fatigue over a weekly schedule.

A family member's voice: similar trust and authenticity benefits, no cost, and can add warmth (for example a grandmother's voice for a bedtime story). Adds coordination overhead (scheduling their time) and consistency risk if availability varies episode to episode.

Kokoro-82M TTS: free (Apache 2.0), fastest per-episode turnaround since there is no recording or re-takes, and licensed for commercial use. English only, so it does not solve Afrikaans or isiZulu. Lower trust and authenticity with parents than a real voice, and synthetic narration sits closer to the "template/generic" pattern YouTube's 16 July 2026 clarification targets, so it raises (not removes) inauthentic-content risk versus a human narrator.

**Recommendation:** Kevin's own voice for English, both for trust and to keep the channel clearly outside the "AI slop" risk bucket. Reserve Kokoro TTS as a fallback only if recording time becomes the bottleneck, and disclose it if used.

### Thumbnail style guide (made-for-kids safe)
**Palette:** night-sky navy `#233255`, dusty lavender `#8B8FC4`, warm cream `#FAF3E7`, honey gold `#E8B04B`, soft blush `#F2B9B0`. Keep contrast high between the character/subject and the background so the thumbnail reads at small sizes.

**Fonts (Google Fonts, free):** "Baloo 2" for the title (rounded, bold, friendly, legible at thumbnail size), "Quicksand" for any secondary text (soft, calm, not shouty), "Nunito" as a fallback body font.

**Layout rule:** Place the main character in one side third (left or right), title text in 3-4 words maximum in the opposite third or along the bottom, set on a soft solid or blurred-background panel for contrast. Never place text across a face.

**Do / don't:**
- Do use soft watercolour-style illustration with rounded shapes. Don't use sharp neon colours, harsh outlines, or photorealistic/AI-realistic faces.
- Do show calm, curious or gently smiling expressions. Don't show crying, fearful or distressed expressions (distress-bait is a policy risk, not just a style choice).
- Do keep text to 3-4 large, legible words. Don't keyword-stuff the thumbnail or clutter it with multiple text elements.
