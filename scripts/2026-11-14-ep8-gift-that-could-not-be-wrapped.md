## Full voiceover script

Narration only below. Sound cues in [brackets]. Visual notes sit under each block. Speak slowly, warmly, like a bedtime storyteller. Pause where marked. Target: about 1000 to 1250 words of narration.

Narrator (Kevin, 2026-09-30): an old wise man with a warm, deep storytelling tone (not young, not neutral). A dedicated voice for each of English, Afrikaans, and isiZulu. Applies to this episode and all future episodes. No voice IDs assigned.

### [0:00-0:30] Hook

[soft morning air; leaves stirring]

It was Giving Day in the Kingdom of Sunhill, and Princess Baylin had a gift for everyone. Every gift was wrapped in a leaf and tied with grass.

Every gift but one.

Because what do you give to a tree?

Come and sit under the old fig tree with us.

**Visual:** Title card over the old fig tree in morning light. A row of small leaf parcels on the grass. Baylin looking up into the branches.

---

### [0:30-1:20] Beat 1 - Giving Day

[birdsong; a soft rustle of leaf parcels]

On the last morning of the old year, Sunhill keeps Giving Day. You give one small thing to each friend who helped you that year.

Baylin had been busy since sunrise. A smooth round pebble for Pip, for the doorway of his reed home. A sprig of mint for Mallow's buns. A soft grey feather she had found, and saved, for the Rainbird's nest.

She laid the parcels in a row and counted her friends on her fingers. Then she counted again. Her finger stopped in the air.

"Tilly," she said. "I forgot the Grandmother Tree."

**Visual:** Baylin kneeling over a neat row of leaf parcels tied with grass. Tilly beside her. One finger raised, mid-count.

---

### [1:20-2:10] Beat 2 - What do you give a tree

[slow tortoise steps; fig leaves whispering]

They went down the path to the old fig tree at the edge of the garden. She was wider than three doors and older than the palace. All year she had given. Shade in the hot months. Figs in the sweet ones. A branch for the Rainbird and a hollow for Bonayo the owl.

Baylin looked up into the green. "What do you give to somebody who gives all year?"

"Something small," said Tilly. "That is the rule."

"Then I shall find the best small thing in the kingdom," said Baylin. And off she went to look.

**Visual:** The Grandmother Tree, huge and kind, filling the frame. Baylin and Tilly very small at her roots, looking up.

---

### [2:10-2:55] Beat 3 - The ribbon

[a ribbon fluttering; a puff of wind]

First she brought a ribbon, as red as a sunbird's chest, and tied it in a bow around the lowest branch.

"There," she said. "Now you are the best-dressed tree in Sunhill."

The Grandmother Tree rustled politely. But a little wind came by, as little winds do. It tugged the bow. It tried it on. And away went the ribbon, over the garden wall.

"Oh," said Baylin.

"The wind likes ribbons more than trees do," said Tilly.

**Visual:** A red ribbon sailing over the garden wall. Baylin watching it go, more surprised than sad.

---

### [2:55-3:45] Beat 4 - The stone

[a small stone set down; tiny beetle feet]

Next she brought a stone from the river, smooth and speckled, and set it between two great roots.

"A tree cannot lose a stone," she said.

But the Grandmother Tree had no pocket to keep it in, and no hand to hold it. By the time Baylin had stepped back to look, a busy beetle had rolled the stone halfway to his own front door.

Baylin sat down on a root. It was harder than she had thought, giving something to a tree.

She had one idea left. It was her biggest one.

**Visual:** A small beetle rolling the speckled stone away between the roots. Gentle and funny, never a loss.

---

### [3:45-4:35] Beat 5 - The empty parcel

[leaves folding; grass string pulled tight]

She fetched the largest leaf in the garden and folded it into a parcel as big as a pumpkin. She tied it with her longest piece of grass. She made a bow with two loops and two tails.

It was the handsomest parcel anyone had ever wrapped.

"What is inside it?" asked Tilly.

Baylin looked at the parcel. The parcel did not help.

"Nothing yet," she said, very quietly. "I wrapped it first. I hoped the gift would come along afterwards."

She put the empty parcel on her knees. "I have nothing a tree can use."

**Visual:** Baylin on a root with a large, beautifully tied, plainly empty leaf parcel on her knees. Puzzled, not upset.

---

### [4:35-5:30] Beat 6 - Who stays

[wind low in the branches; a long quiet]

Tilly settled down beside her, slowly, the way tortoises settle.

"What does the Grandmother Tree do all day?" she asked.

Baylin thought. "She stays."

"And who stays with her?"

Baylin thought again. The frogs came for the shade and hopped away. The birds came for the figs and flew away. She herself came every single day, and ran on to somewhere else.

"Nobody," she said.

Above them the leaves moved, though there was no wind at all.

"I have watched this garden for a hundred summers," said the Grandmother Tree, in her voice like quiet wood. "Everybody passes. Will you sit a while?"

**Visual:** Looking up through the leaves from Baylin's place at the roots. Small shapes of frogs and birds coming and going at the edges.

---

### [5:30-6:25] Beat 7 - Stay, soft and close (child pause)

[gentle underscore]

[On-screen cue: "Stay... soft and close"]

So Baylin put down the empty parcel. She leaned her back against the great warm trunk. And she stayed.

She did not fetch anything. She did not fix anything. She only sat, close enough to hear the tree breathing in her leaves.

Now it is your turn, little listener. Is there somebody you could sit beside for a little while? You do not have to say anything at all.

[4 to 5 second pause for the child]

Stay... soft and close.

That is all it takes.

**Visual:** Baylin leaning against the trunk, eyes half closed, Tilly beside her. Hold on the cue text through the pause.

---

### [6:25-7:20] Beat 8 - The river brought to her roots

[a river sound, then a market hush, soft as a memory]

After a while, Baylin began to talk. Not loudly. The way you talk to somebody you love when the day is slow.

"You cannot walk to the river, Grandmother Tree," she said. "So I will bring the river to you."

She told her how the water sounds where Pip lives, like somebody whispering a secret. She told her how the market smells in the morning, of warm buns and dust. She told her how a hundred lanterns look when the whole hill is thanking somebody.

The Grandmother Tree held every leaf still, so as not to miss a word.

**Visual:** Small sketched pictures of the river, the market and the lantern hill drifting up among the leaves as Baylin talks.

---

### [7:20-8:05] Beat 9 - Sweeping the roots

[a soft brushing sound; leaves gathered]

When the stories were told, Baylin saw that old leaves had drifted deep between the roots. So she swept them out with her hands, one root at a time, the way you would brush a grandmother's hair.

It was slow work. It was not exciting work. Tilly helped with the corners.

"That is better," sighed the Grandmother Tree. "I have had an itch between those two roots since the rains."

**Visual:** Baylin sweeping leaves from between the great roots with her hands. Tilly nudging a leaf out of a corner.

---

### [8:05-8:50] Beat 10 - The last fig

[one soft plop; warm afternoon hum]

The afternoon turned gold. Baylin leaned against the trunk again, and the bark was warm, like a hand.

Plop.

Something soft landed in her lap. It was a fig, the last ripe one of the year.

Baylin laughed. "Grandmother Tree! It is your Giving Day. You are not supposed to give me anything."

"I cannot help it," said the tree. "Giving is what I do. But today, for once, somebody gave to me."

**Visual:** Gold afternoon light. One ripe fig in Baylin's lap. Her delighted face turned up to the branches.

---

### [8:50-9:45] Beat 11 - What did you give her

[friends arriving; soft chatter; parcels rustling]

As the sun slid down, the friends came along the path with their own small parcels, to swap beneath the fig tree as they did every year.

The Rainbird put her head on one side. "I do not see a parcel for the Grandmother Tree," she said. "What did you give her, Princess?"

Baylin looked at the empty leaf parcel, still lying where she had left it. She did not know what to say.

Tilly said it for her. "The afternoon."

"It was the best gift in a hundred summers," said the Grandmother Tree. "It could not be wrapped. It could only be given."

**Visual:** Friends gathered under the tree at sunset with small parcels. The empty leaf parcel in the grass. Tilly looking quietly pleased.

---

### [9:45-10:15] Beat 12 - Bonayo and home

[soft owl call; slow footsteps; bedsheets]

High in the hollow, somebody was waking up. "Hoo-hoo."

"Well stayed, Princess Baylin," said Bonayo the owl.

Baylin gave out her parcels, one small thing for each friend. Then she walked home through the blue evening and climbed into bed.

"Thank you, friends!" she whispered.

[On-screen: "Thank you, friends!" / AF "Dankie, vriende!" / ZU "Siyabonga, bangane!" - AF/ZU NEEDS NATIVE-SPEAKER CHECK]

**Visual:** Bonayo blinking awake in the hollow of the fig tree. Then Baylin's window in blue evening light, and the thank-you card.

---

### [10:15-10:50] Beat 13 - Goodnight (slow)

[music slows almost to nothing]

And you, little listener. Tomorrow, you could give somebody a little of your time. It is the one gift everybody has, and nobody can wrap.

Next time, on another calm night, there is a story called Princess Baylin and the Knot That Grew.

Goodnight. Sleep well. The old fig tree will keep a soft watch while you dream.

[fade out]

**Visual:** Slow pull back from the window to the old fig tree against the night sky. Disclosure card: Created from Kevin's stories, brought to life with AI.
