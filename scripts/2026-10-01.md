## Full voiceover script

Narration only below. Sound cues in [brackets]. Visual notes sit under each block. Speak slowly, warmly, like a bedtime storyteller. Pause where marked. Target: about 1000 to 1250 words of narration.

Narrator (Kevin, 2026-09-30): an old wise man with a warm, deep storytelling tone (not young, not neutral). A dedicated voice for each of English, Afrikaans, and isiZulu. Applies to this episode and all future episodes. No voice IDs assigned.

### [0:00-0:20] Hook

[soft river hush; light splash of shallow water]

Princess Baylin raced ahead of Tilly along the riverbank and splashed straight through a tiny home of reeds and pebbles. "Sorry!" she called over her shoulder, and kept running. But a small voice bubbled up from the water: "My home... it's all come apart."

Come with us to a sunlit river at the edge of the Kingdom of Sunhill, where a hurried sorry learns to slow down, and kind hands help mend what broke.

**Visual:** Title card fades in over a bright morning river. Baylin mid-run looking back. Scattered pebbles. Pip's tiny face peeks from pale blue water. Calm faces.

---

### [0:20-1:10] Beat 1 - Race begins

[soft footsteps on sand; gentle river murmur; morning birds far away]

Once upon a bright morning, when the sun was warm on the reeds, Princess Baylin and Tilly the tortoise walked the sandy path beside the river.

The water whispered softly against the pebbles.

Baylin bounced on her toes. The willow tree stood ahead, green and inviting.

"Race you to the willow!" she called, already leaning forward.

Tilly smiled her slow smile. "I will come at my own pace," she said. "The river does not mind waiting."

But Baylin was already off, feet light on the warm sand.

**Visual:** Sunlit riverbank. Green reeds. Pale blue water. Warm sandy path. Willow ahead. Baylin starting to run. Tilly calm and slow. Bright morning palette (first daytime setting in the series).

---

### [1:10-2:00] Beat 2 - The accident

[quick splash through shallow water; soft scatter of pebbles; reeds rustle]

Baylin's path dipped into the shallows where the reeds grew thick and friendly.

She did not see the tiny home woven there, small as two cupped hands. Reeds laced through pebbles. A little door of smooth stones.

Splash. Her feet rushed straight through.

Pebbles rolled. Reeds loosened. The tiny home came apart.

Baylin felt a bump under her toes and kept going. The willow was so close.

Behind her, the river whispered a little sadder than before.

**Visual:** Shallow reeds. Tiny reed-and-pebble home. Baylin's feet splash through. Soft scatter of pebbles (never violent). No danger framing. Daylight clear and gentle.

---

### [2:00-2:50] Beat 3 - A sad voice

[soft bubble rise; quiet sad (not angry) fish voice; Baylin call fading ahead]

From the pale blue water, a small face peeked up. It was Pip the river fish, tiny and gentle, looking at the scattered reeds and pebbles that used to be home.

"My home... it's all come apart," Pip said. Not angry. Just sad.

Up ahead, Baylin called over her shoulder without slowing.

"Sorry!"

The word floated back on the breeze, light and quick, and Baylin kept running toward the willow.

Pip looked at one pebble that had rolled away. His fins moved once, then went still.

**Visual:** Pip peeking from water, sad not angry. Broken reed home. Baylin small in the distance, still running. Soft comic hurry, never distress-bait.

---

### [2:50-3:40] Beat 4 - Tilly catches up

[slow tortoise shuffle; river hush; Baylin footsteps slow then stop]

Tilly reached the reeds at her own calm pace. She looked at the mess. She looked at Pip. She looked toward the willow, where Baylin was already turning with a proud little wave.

"Baylin," Tilly called gently. "Will you come back a moment?"

Baylin jogged back a few steps, still smiling from the race.

Tilly tipped her head toward the scattered pebbles.

"I think your sorry ran away before you did."

Baylin blinked. "I said sorry," she said. But her voice was smaller now, as if the word itself felt thin.

**Visual:** Tilly at the reed edge. Pip still sad. Baylin returning a few steps. Warm sandy bank. Soft teaching beat, no scolding faces.

---

### [3:40-4:30] Beat 5 - Baylin realises

[soft pause; river whisper; Baylin footsteps settle]

Baylin stopped fully. She looked back. Really looked.

Pip was still there, tiny in the shallows, staring at the loose reeds.

Baylin's cheeks went warm.

She remembered how hard it had been to wait for a moon that would not hurry, and how a quiet star had needed a soft blink, not a parade.

"Oh," Baylin whispered. "I said the word. But I did not stay."

Tilly waited beside her, as steady as a stone in the sun.

**Visual:** Close on Baylin looking back. Warm cheeks. Pip small and sad. Tilly steady. Soft emotional beat, no tears bait.

---

### [4:30-5:20] Beat 6 - Baylin returns

[slow careful footsteps back to water; soft kneel on sand]

Baylin walked back slowly this time. No race. No hurry.

She knelt at the edge of the shallows so her face was closer to Pip's.

"I am sorry," she said again. Softer. Truer. "I was racing and I did not see your home. What broke? How can I help?"

Pip's fins fluttered once. He still looked sad, but a little less alone.

"The pebbles rolled away," Pip said. "The reeds came loose. It was a small home. But it was mine."

Baylin nodded. "Then we will mend it. Soft and true."

**Visual:** Baylin kneeling at water's edge. Pip closer. Tilly nearby. Shallows clear and safe. No drowning risk framing. Calm help-offer beat.

---

### [5:20-6:10] Beat 7 - Mend soft and true (child pause)

[soft pebble place in water; gentle reed dip]

[On-screen cue: "Mend... soft and true"]

[3-5 second pause for the child to whisper or place a soft "sorry" along]

Pip showed Baylin where the first pebble belonged. Not tossed. Placed. The way you set a cup down when someone is sleeping nearby.

Baylin put the first pebble slowly.

"Mend... soft and true," she whispered.

Can you mend with Baylin too? Soft. True. One careful pebble. A real sorry has hands that help, not just words that hurry past.

Pip watched. For the first time since the splash, a tiny hopeful light touched his eyes.

**Visual:** Close on Baylin placing first pebble. Mid-story child pause with on-screen cue. Soft sunlit water. Pip watching. Pale blue, green reed, warm sand.

---

### [6:10-7:00] Beat 8 - Working together

[soft reed hand-off; gentle weaving sounds; tortoise shuffle closer]

Tilly moved closer and picked up a loose reed with care.

"One at a time," Tilly said. "Rushing broke it. Slow can build it."

She handed Baylin reeds one by one. Baylin wove them gently between the pebbles the way Pip showed her, under and over.

Sometimes Baylin wanted to go faster. Then she took a breath, the way the river breathes against the bank, and slowed again.

Pip guided with tiny nods. Left a little. Right a little. Yes. There.

The willow waited patiently, as if it had never minded the unfinished race at all.

**Visual:** Three-shot: Baylin weaving, Tilly handing reeds, Pip guiding. Soft teamwork. Daytime bright and warm. No frantic montage.

---

### [7:00-7:50] Beat 9 - The home returns

[last soft pebble settle; gentle water slip through reeds; soft whisper sound]

The last pebble settled into place. Baylin drew her hands back and held her breath.

Then the current slipped through the woven reeds with a soft whispering sound, as if the river itself was saying, yes, this is home again.

Pip swam a small circle around his doorway. The reeds held. The pebbles stayed. The tiny home looked like itself once more.

Baylin smiled the way she smiles when a hard thing turns gentle.

"It whispers again," she said.

"It does," said Pip. "Thank you for staying."

**Visual:** Finished reed-and-pebble home. Soft water whisper through reeds. Pip circling calmly. Baylin and Tilly watching. Warm midday light.

---

### [7:50-8:40] Beat 10 - Pip's lesson

[warm soft pad; gentle river underscore]

Pip rested near a smooth stone and looked up at Baylin.

"A true sorry has hands that help," Pip said, "not just words that hurry past."

Baylin nodded, remembering it the way you remember a song you want to keep.

"I will try to slow down when I say sorry," she said. "And ask what needs mending."

Tilly's eyes were kind. "That is a brave kind of slow," she said. "The river knows it well."

**Visual:** Pip lesson close-up. Baylin nodding. Tilly kind. Soft emotional landing. No lecture framing. Daytime gold on water.

---

### [8:40-9:20] Beat 11 - Thanks by the river

[soft breeze in willow; evening-tint wash beginning; gentle thanks]

As the light grew softer and the day leaned toward evening, Baylin stood and pressed her hands together in thanks.

"Thank you, Tilly. Thank you, Pip."

Pip gave a tiny happy splash.

"Thank you for coming back," Pip said.

Baylin looked at the willow she had been racing toward. It was still there, green and patient. She did not need to race it now.

The river whispered on, soft and whole.

**Visual:** Evening wash beginning on riverbank. Thanks beat. Pip happy splash (tiny). Willow patient in background. Warm peach tipping into soft gold.

---

### [9:20-10:00] Beat 12 - Home and soft sleep + thank-you + Ep 5 tease

[music slows; soft footsteps home; bedsheets; fade toward hush]

They walked home calmly along the sandy path. No hurry now. Only the memory of pebbles placed soft and true, and a sorry that learned to stay.

Back in her room, Baylin climbed into bed. Her eyes felt heavy in the nicest way. Outside the window, the river kept its quiet whisper.

"Thank you, friends!" said Baylin.

[On-screen: "Thank you, friends!" / AF "Dankie, vriende!" / ZU "Siyabonga, bangane!" - AF/ZU NEEDS NATIVE-SPEAKER CHECK]

Then Baylin tipped her face toward the soft dark and whispered goodnight, the way she always does when a story is done.

What could you help mend today? Perhaps a toy set gently back. Perhaps a kind word that stays.

And if you listen very carefully on another calm day, you may hear a gentle story called Princess Baylin and the Very Patient Tortoise, when kindness takes its time again.

Goodnight, little listener. Sleep well. The whispering river will keep a soft watch while you dream.

[fade out]

**Visual:** Warm interior. Soft evening through the window. Distant river silhouette. Baylin's eyes closing. Thank-you ritual card. Optional soft tortoise silhouette tease for a later episode. No hard sell, no end-screen style push.
