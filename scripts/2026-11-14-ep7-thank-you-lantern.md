## Full voiceover script

Narration only below. Sound cues in [brackets]. Visual notes sit under each block. Speak slowly, warmly, like a bedtime storyteller. Pause where marked. Target: about 1000 to 1250 words of narration.

Narrator (Kevin, 2026-09-30): an old wise man with a warm, deep storytelling tone (not young, not neutral). A dedicated voice for each of English, Afrikaans, and isiZulu. Applies to this episode and all future episodes. No voice IDs assigned.

### [0:00-0:30] Hook

[soft evening hush; one paper lantern creaks]

Once a year, when the old year is nearly done, the Kingdom of Sunhill keeps Lantern Night.

A hundred paper lanterns wait along the hill path. Each one lights only for a thank-you.

Princess Baylin had the biggest thank-you in the kingdom ready. And not one lantern glowed.

Come and walk the lantern path with us.

**Visual:** Title card over the dusk hill path. Rows of unlit paper lanterns. Baylin small against the evening sky.

---

### [0:30-1:20] Beat 1 - The lantern keeper

[crickets; small careful footsteps]

Evening came softly to Sunhill. All along the hill path, from the palace steps to the old fig tree, hung a hundred paper lanterns, round and pale as sleeping moons.

A small hedgehog walked beneath them with a tall reed in his paws. This was Wick, the lantern keeper. He straightened each lantern with great care. But his reed had no flame on it at all.

"Wick," said Princess Baylin, "how will you light them without a flame?"

"I do not light them," said Wick. "Nobody does. They light themselves, when they hear what they are waiting for."

**Visual:** Wick with his reed under the lanterns. Baylin and Tilly watching. Soft blue dusk, no light yet.

---

### [1:20-2:10] Beat 2 - The rule

[paper lanterns swaying]

"What are they waiting for?" asked Baylin.

Wick touched the nearest lantern with the tip of his reed. "A thank-you," he said. "These are thank-you lanterns. One true thank-you, one lantern."

Tilly the tortoise blinked one slow blink. "A hundred lanterns," she said. "That is a great many thank-yous."

Baylin clapped her hands. "Then I know just what to do," she said. "I will say one thank-you big enough for all of them!"

Wick opened his mouth to say something. Then he closed it again and smiled a small hedgehog smile. Some things are better found than told.

**Visual:** Close on Wick's reed touching a lantern. Baylin delighted. Wick's small knowing smile.

---

### [2:10-2:55] Beat 3 - One big thank-you

[footsteps on stone; a held breath]

Baylin climbed to the top of the palace steps. She did not run. She stood up tall, the way you do for something important, and spread her arms wide enough to hold the whole kingdom.

"Thank you, everyone," she called, "for everything!"

Her voice rolled out over the garden and the river and the market, and came back again. Everything. Everything.

The lanterns swayed a little in the evening air.

And stayed dark.

**Visual:** Wide shot from behind Baylin, arms spread, the dark lantern path below. Calm, not sad.

---

### [2:55-3:50] Beat 4 - A thank-you with no name

[quiet; one cricket]

Baylin said it again, with a bow. She said it a third time, as a song. The lanterns only swayed.

"It was a very big thank-you," she said. Her voice had gone small.

"It was," said Wick kindly. "But it had no name in it. A thank-you with no name does not know which way to go. It floats about and lands nowhere."

Tilly came slowly up the steps and sat down beside her. "Who helped you this year, Baylin?" she asked. "Do not think of everyone. Think of one."

Baylin thought. And a soft grey-blue feather came into her mind.

**Visual:** Baylin sitting on the step, puzzled, not upset. Tilly beside her. A single grey-blue feather drawn as a thought above her.

---

### [3:50-4:35] Beat 5 - The first lantern

[leaves; one soft bird note]

They walked down the path to the old fig tree, where the Rainbird sat with her head tucked under her wing.

"Rainbird," said Baylin, quietly now. "Thank you for singing the rain song home, on the day the garden was thirsty."

The Rainbird lifted her head. Her eyes shone.

And just above them, one paper lantern gave a tiny flicker. Then a glow. Then a warm gold light, soft as honey.

"Oh," whispered Baylin.

"One name," said Wick. "One true thing."

**Visual:** The first lantern lighting above the Rainbird. Warm gold on blue, the first colour change in the episode.

---

### [4:35-5:30] Beat 6 - The river and the market

[water lapping; then a soft market hush]

Now Baylin knew the way. And she did not hurry it.

At the river she knelt on the bank. "Pip, thank you for showing me that a real sorry has hands that help." Down by the water, a lantern woke.

At the market she found a small mouse with flour on her whiskers. "Mallow, thank you for the warm bun, and for telling me that small is enough." Above the stall, a lantern woke.

Two more lights. And each one, Baylin noticed, felt different to say. No two thank-yous were the same, because no two friends were.

**Visual:** Two short scenes. Pip's lantern reflected in the river. Mallow's lantern over the stall.

---

### [5:30-6:20] Beat 7 - Thank, soft and warm (child pause)

[gentle underscore]

[On-screen cue: "Thank... soft and warm"]

High above the path, a small shy star was peeping round a cloud.

"Quiet Star," Baylin called softly. "Thank you for shining small, so the firefly could find the way home."

The highest lantern on the hill began to glow.

Now it is your turn, little listener. Who helped you today? Say their name. Say one true thing.

[4 to 5 second pause for the child]

Thank... soft and warm.

Did you feel that? That small warm glow? That is your lantern.

**Visual:** The Quiet Star and the highest lantern. Hold on the cue text through the pause.

---

### [6:20-7:05] Beat 8 - The thank-yous travel

[small voices, far off and gentle]

Then something happened that Baylin had not planned at all.

The Rainbird turned to the frogs. "Thank you for the drum." A lantern lit beside the pond.

Mallow turned to the bees. "Thank you for the honey in my buns." A lantern lit beside the hives.

Pip thanked the reeds for his roof. The grasshoppers thanked the grass.

All across Sunhill, thank-yous were finding their names. And lantern by lantern, the dark hill turned into a river of small gold lights.

**Visual:** Wide view of the hill, lanterns lighting one after another. The key image of the episode.

---

### [7:05-7:50] Beat 9 - Tilly's lantern

[quiet; slow tortoise steps]

Baylin turned to the friend who had walked beside her all evening. All year.

This one was hard. How do you thank someone for every day?

She thought of one true thing.

"Tilly," she said. "Thank you for waiting for me. Every single time."

Tilly did not say anything. Her eyes shone slowly, the way tortoise eyes do.

And the lantern nearest to them glowed the warmest of all.

**Visual:** Close two-shot of Baylin and Tilly under one lantern. The warmest light in the episode.

---

### [7:50-8:45] Beat 10 - One lantern left

[night air; one lantern creaking]

At the very top of the path, beside the palace steps, one lantern was still dark.

Baylin thanked the hill for its path. She thanked the evening for being gentle. She thanked Wick for keeping the lanterns, and a lantern lit far down the hill, and Wick went pink to the tips of his prickles.

But the last lantern stayed dark.

"I have thanked everyone I can think of," said Baylin. "Why will it not light?"

Wick smiled his small smile. "Because that one," he said, "is not yours to light."

**Visual:** The one dark lantern at the top of a glowing path. Wick blushing. Curious, not worried.

---

### [8:45-9:30] Beat 11 - The last lantern

[hush; then voices, one at a time]

Tilly looked up at Baylin.

"Thank you, Baylin," she said, "for learning to listen."

"Thank you," called Pip from the river, "for coming back to mend my home."

"Thank you," said Mallow, "for sharing small."

And the last lantern, the one beside the palace steps, filled with light.

It was Baylin's lantern. She had never once thought to count herself. She felt warm from her bare toes to her crown of grass and flowers.

**Visual:** The last lantern lighting above Baylin. Friends gathered below. Her surprised, glad face.

---

### [9:30-10:00] Beat 12 - Bonayo and home

[soft owl call; slow footsteps; bedsheets]

"Hoo-hoo." From the old fig tree, Bonayo the owl looked out over a hundred small gold lights.

"Well thanked, Princess Baylin," he said.

Baylin walked home under the lanterns and climbed into bed with their glow still on her window.

"Thank you, friends!" she whispered.

[On-screen: "Thank you, friends!" / AF "Dankie, vriende!" / ZU "Siyabonga, bangane!" - AF/ZU NEEDS NATIVE-SPEAKER CHECK]

**Visual:** Bonayo in the fig tree above the lit hill. Then Baylin's window with lantern glow, and the thank-you card.

---

### [10:00-10:30] Beat 13 - Goodnight (slow)

[music slows almost to nothing]

And you, little listener. Tomorrow you could light a lantern of your own. One name. One true thing.

Next time, on another calm night, there is a story called Princess Baylin and the Gift That Could Not Be Wrapped.

Goodnight. Sleep well. The lanterns will keep a soft watch while you dream.

[fade out]

**Visual:** Slow pull back from the window to the whole glowing hill. Disclosure card: Created from Kevin's stories, brought to life with AI.
