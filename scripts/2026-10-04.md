### [0:00-0:20] Hook

[soft wind, very quiet garden ambience]

The garden flowers were drooping, the pond was almost empty, and the Rainbird had forgotten the song that brings the rain. Princess Baylin thought she could fix it by singing louder. She was wrong.

Tonight we walk with Baylin through a dry morning in the Kingdom of Sunhill, and we learn how a lost song finds its way home.

**Visual:** Title card fades in over pale sky and drooping flowers. Soft watercolour. Calm faces.

---

### [0:20-1:40] Beat 1 - Dry morning

[quiet footsteps on dry grass]

Once upon a soft morning, when the sun was kind and the air felt dusty, Princess Baylin stepped into the palace garden with her friend Tilly the tortoise.

"Oh," whispered Baylin. "Look at the flowers."

The flowers had hung their little heads. Their leaves felt thirsty. The pond, which usually sparkled like a mirror, was almost empty. A frog sat on a lily pad that looked too big for the water left underneath.

Tilly blinked slowly. Tortoises do everything slowly, and that is part of their magic.

"It has not rained for a long time," said Tilly. "Even the grass is whispering for a drink."

Baylin knelt and touched a petal with one careful finger. "Don't worry," she said, trying to sound brave. "We will find a way to help."

**Visual:** Wide garden shot. Drooping flowers, low pond, Baylin and Tilly side by side. Warm greens turning dusty gold.

---

### [1:40-2:45] Beat 2 - Sad Rainbird

[soft bird rustle, no sharp cries]

They followed a dry path to the old fig tree at the edge of the garden. High on a branch sat the Rainbird. Her feathers were soft and grey-blue, and her eyes looked tired.

"Hello, Rainbird," said Baylin gently. "Why do you look so sad?"

The Rainbird sighed a little sigh. "I've forgotten my rain song," she said. "Without my song, the clouds don't know when to gather. Without the rain, the garden grows thirsty. I have tried and tried, but the tune will not come."

Baylin's heart felt a small squeeze. She loved her garden. She loved her friends. And she wanted to fix things quickly.

"I can help!" she said. "I will sing the rain song for you."

**Visual:** Rainbird on fig branch, soft sad expression (friendly, not frightening). Baylin looking up. Tilly nearby.

---

### [2:45-3:50] Beat 3 - Baylin tries loud

[Baylin runs; light comic dust puff; no thunder]

Baylin ran to the hilltop as fast as her feet would carry her. The wind brushed her hair. She stood tall, filled her lungs, and sang as loudly as she could.

"Come rain! Come rain! Come rain!" she called, making up a song of her own.

She sang higher. She sang louder. She even stamped one foot for extra power.

And then... [tiny dust puff sound]

A little puff of dust rose from the dry ground. That was all. No clouds. No rain. Only dust that made Baylin cough a tiny cough.

She sat down hard on the hill. "Why didn't it work?" she asked. Her cheeks felt warm. She was frustrated, but she was still safe, and the day was still gentle.

**Visual:** Hilltop under pale sky. Baylin singing big. Small comic dust puff. Soft expression of frustration, never distress-bait.

---

### [3:50-4:40] Beat 4 - Tilly: listen, not shout

[pause; soft breeze]

Tilly arrived at last, one careful step after another.

"You sang very bravely," said Tilly. "But maybe we need to listen, not shout."

Baylin frowned. "Listen to what?"

"To the garden," said Tilly. "To our friends. Sometimes a song is not kept in one place. Sometimes it is shared out, in little pieces, and we only hear it when we are quiet enough."

Baylin took a slow breath. She closed her eyes for a moment.

[2-3 second pause for the child]

Listen... what do you hear?

[On-screen cue: "Listen... what do you hear?"]

Perhaps you hear the wind. Perhaps you hear a bird. In Sunhill, Baylin began to hear something too: the soft wish of a thirsty garden, waiting to be heard.

**Visual:** Close on Baylin and Tilly. Soft listening pause. On-screen mid cue text (EN; AF/ZU only after native check).

---

### [4:40-5:25] Beat 5 - Frogs = drum

[soft frog "drum" rhythm, gentle, not startling]

First they visited the pond. The frogs sat on their lily pads like little green musicians.

"Please," said Baylin, much more softly now. "Do you remember any part of the rain song?"

The biggest frog smiled. "We remember the drum," he said. "The deep, slow beat when raindrops fall on the water. Boom. Boom. Boom."

[soft low drum taps, slow]

Baylin nodded. "Thank you. That is a wonderful piece."

She did not rush away. She waited, and she listened, until the frog drum lived in her ears.

**Visual:** Pond, lily pads, playful frogs. Soft drum motif as light animation on ripples.

---

### [5:25-6:10] Beat 6 - Grasshoppers = patter

[light tickly patter rhythm]

Next they walked through the long grass. Grasshoppers leapt like tiny sparks of gold.

"Hello, grasshoppers," whispered Baylin. "Do you remember any part of the rain song?"

"We remember the patter," chirped one. "The quick, light taps when rain kisses the leaves. Tip. Tip. Tip-tip."

[light patter SFX]

Baylin smiled. "The drum and the patter. Two pieces already."

Tilly's eyes shone slowly, the way tortoise eyes do when hope is growing.

**Visual:** Long grass, grasshoppers, light tickly motion. Baylin kneeling to listen at child height.

---

### [6:10-7:00] Beat 7 - Grandmother tree = hum of wind

[soft wind hum through leaves]

At last they returned to the old fig tree. Baylin placed her hand on the warm bark.

"Grandmother tree," she said, "do you remember any part of the rain song?"

The tree swayed kindly. Her leaves whispered like pages turning.

"I remember the hum of the wind," she said, in a voice like quiet wood. "The long soft note that moves before the rain arrives. Hmmm..."

[soft sustained hum]

Baylin closed her eyes again. Drum. Patter. Hum. Three pieces, held carefully in her heart.

**Visual:** Grandmother fig tree, kindly face suggested in bark/leaves without being scary. Soft wind particles.

---

### [7:00-7:45] Beat 8 - Almost giving up

[music thins; still calm]

They brought the pieces back to the Rainbird. Baylin tried to put them in order. Drum, then patter, then hum. Hum, then drum, then patter. Nothing quite fitted. The Rainbird shook her head gently.

"I still can't hear the tune," she said.

Baylin's shoulders dropped. "Maybe I am not enough," she whispered.

Tilly bumped Baylin's ankle with her shell, soft as a hug.

"Tortoises get there in the end," said Tilly. "We do not win by being first. We win by staying kind, and by staying with our friends."

Baylin looked at the frogs. She looked at the grasshoppers. She looked at the grandmother tree. And she looked at the Rainbird.

"Then we will try together," she said.

**Visual:** Group gathered under the fig. Baylin briefly discouraged, then softening as friends gather close.

---

### [7:45-8:45] Beat 9 - Song comes together

[layers: frog drum + grasshopper patter + wind hum; Rainbird melody enters softly]

"Ready?" asked Baylin.

The frogs began the drum. Boom. Boom. Boom.
The grasshoppers added the patter. Tip. Tip. Tip-tip.
The grandmother tree offered the hum of the wind. Hmmm...

And then, like a door opening in the sky, the Rainbird remembered.

Her voice found the missing tune. It was not loud. It was not showy. It was true. It wove the drum and the patter and the hum into one gentle rain song.

Baylin did not shout over them. She listened. She hummed along, softly, so everyone could still be heard.

**Visual:** Friends making music together. Soft glow. Rainbird lifting her head with quiet joy.

---

### [8:45-9:30] Beats 10-11 - Soft rain; puddle joy and thanks

[gentle rain begins; no thunder; soft puddle sounds]

Above them, clouds gathered like soft grey blankets. Then came the rain. Soft drops. Kind drops. Drops that made tiny circles in the pond and tiny crowns on the dusty path.

The flowers lifted their heads. The grass drank. The frogs laughed their happy frog laughs. Baylin held out her hands and felt the cool rain on her palms.

"The song was never lost," she said, smiling. "It was shared out. Each of you carried a small piece of the answer."

She thanked the frogs for the drum. She thanked the grasshoppers for the patter. She thanked the grandmother tree for the hum. She thanked Tilly for teaching her to listen. And she thanked the Rainbird for singing the tune home.

**Visual:** Soft rain, lifting flowers, puddle joy. Warm blues joining the greens and golds. No storms.

---

### [9:30-10:00] Beat 12 - Bedtime close + Bonayo soft goodnight + soft Ep 2 nod

[music slows; rain fades to hush; soft owl call]

As the light grew soft and sleepy, Baylin and her friends stood together in the gentle rain.

High in the fig, a familiar soft shape settled on a branch. Bonayo the owl blinked his round wise eyes.

"Hoo-hoo," called Bonayo, warm as old wood. "Well listened, Princess Baylin."

Baylin pressed her hands together in thanks.

"Thank you, friends!" said Baylin.

[On-screen: "Thank you, friends!" / AF "Dankie, vriende!" / ZU "Siyabonga, bangane!" - AF/ZU NEEDS NATIVE-SPEAKER CHECK]

Then Baylin tipped her face to the sky and whispered goodnight, the way she always does when a story is done.

What small thing can you do to help your friends today? Perhaps you can listen. Listening is a kind of love.

And if you listen very carefully on another calm night, you may hear another quiet adventure, when Princess Baylin meets the Sleepy Moon, and learns that waiting can be as brave as running. (Ep 2 already approved as a next watch; no spoilers.)

Goodnight, little listener. Sleep well. The rain will keep singing softly while you dream.

[fade out]

**Visual:** Soft fade. Thank-you ritual card. Bonayo soft goodnight on the fig (named; never leave the owl unnamed if shown). Baylin whispering to the sky. Optional gentle moon silhouette nod for Episode 2. No hard sell, no end-screen style push. No Episode 7 tease in this pack.
